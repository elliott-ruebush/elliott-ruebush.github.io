#### Wildlife (4 out of the big 5 in one trip is pretty good!)
They like to talk about the big 5 large mammals up here: moose, caribou, dall sheep, bear, and wolf. We saw all of them except the wolf, which I have yet to see all summer.

We didn't have too many wildlife sitings while out n' about on the bikes (maybe it was too hot?), just a few caribou here and there and a moose munchin greens off the road.

However, we got some great views of wildlife (although the sheep were so far off their only differentiator from being a rock was that they moved up hill) from the bus rides out to East Fork (the start of the detour around the landslide) and back.

### Soundscapes of Alaska
I have some fun new digital content for you this week! 

As part of the many thousands of hours of audio data we've collected over the years, we end up encountering all sorts of interesting natural sounds. To help out with interpretive and educational programs (and also just because it's really interesting), I've been going through some of the highlights that my supervisor has saved over the years and developing a website to showcase them publicly. These sounds come from National Parks all across Alaska, so they capture environments ranging from coastal fjords to boreal forest to glaciated alpine peaks!

Please take a scroll through at https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/ (mobile or desktop works)

Note: this site also has accessibility features for screen reader use, if you're a screen reader user please tell me how navigable the site is. I'm looking to improve the UI/UX for all users.

I've included some personal favorites, but feel free to explore more - and check out the [about page](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/about/) for more details on the spectrogram visualization. I'm actively going through our audio data and working on improving the site, so let me know if there are any sounds you'd like me to try to find or any suggestions on the design.

Otherwise, happy soundscape listening! Throw on some headphones or hook up a speaker, then sit back, listen, and transport yourself up to Alaska via a medium beyond my text or images!

[A variety of raven calls ](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/denahots_20140602_085834/) These dudes make all sorts of interesting sounds, but the "water droplet" call at ~46 seconds is an especially cool one. [More ravens here!](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/denasush_20260610_092353/)

[Varied Thrush songs with a Swainson's Thrush singing in the background.](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/denatrla_20260610_005302/). The varied thrush was an iconic sound ringing through the forest when I started my season up here! It's the first bird song I learned to identify (not that I can identify that many more currently lol).

[Humpback Whale song and a Sooty Grouse](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/glbabartc_20210628_065656/). This one is from Glacier Bay - my supervisor played it in our little office with the subwoofer turned up and the whale song was such a fascinating sound to feel reverberate around the room.

[Crisp, close thunder up the ridgeline](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/denasush_20260612_111248/). Not unique to Alaska, but this one is from a sound station deployment I helped with this summer, so I've gotta shout it out! The second raven call link above is also from the same site this year.

[Rock motion on a glacier](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/denauyen_20120521_144128/). This is such a satisfying, rumbly sound. You might be able to make an estimate of the size of these boulders based on the spectral composition of the sound - or you could just picture them as big ol' rocks!

[Coyotes making spooky echo-y howls](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/denatrla_20170712_233940/). I mean, a wolf is probably more intimidating, but the sounds these coyotes make are pretty haunting.

[An absolutely classic wolf pack howling](https://mertensia.github.io/alaska_acoustic_highlights/clips/wrstblmt_20170128_072543/). From Wrangell-St. Elias - this one actually gave me chills when I heard it for the first time. Plus the spectrogram looks beautiful.
