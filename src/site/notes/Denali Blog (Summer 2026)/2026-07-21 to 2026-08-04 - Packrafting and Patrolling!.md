### Packrafting

#### Gear Test
I finally got out for a maiden voyage with my set of personal packrafting gear. First, to check things were in order, I went out with some friends on a local lake. I figured I should make sure the boat floated and also test out just how dry my *mostly*-drysuit was. Answer = mostly dry.
![Pasted image 20260807192704.png](/img/user/Pasted%20image%2020260807192704.png)
On the water! I suppose I could claim this was super remote and was a crazy expedition ... but we drove right up and this was probably a few hundred feet from the car.

#### Proper Day Out
The next week, I had the chance to get out with a group of coworkers for a proper Alaskan packrafting experience. We packed up our gear, hiked up an offroading trail, bushwhacked while wading in a stream, and generally meandered our way up a steep, alongside a creek. Then, we put in and had a nice float all the way down to the larger river in the valley. Along the way, we had a slightly sketchy run-in with a downed tree across the stream and enjoyed some splashy, relatively chill sections of whitewater that were all good fun!

Although I was a very sleepy fellow upon my return, we had amazing luck with the weather (it was almost TOO hot with sunny days pushing 70 degrees) and were able to complete all of our objectives on the patrol. Traversing the Alaska Range provided a unique opportunity to observe the changes in our surroundings as we went up and over the passes. As you can see from the sampling of pictures I've supplied, there's a wide variety of ecosystems and environments to be admired up here.

However, you don't need to come to Alaska to get out there and appreciate both the big and small features of the land around you! There are cool flowers, wacky wildlife, high points to climb, and adventures to be had at home too!

(I do really appreciate the effects of some topography on the landscape though - sorry Illinois...)

Get out there and see what you can see in the land around you this week!
